Opening RepoFluent

There is no sign-in page. You open the address, and you are already inside as one of five identities. Which identity you are is entirely under your control, and changing it changes what you can see and do. Understanding this is the most important thing on your first day.

The banner at the top of every page

The first thing you will see is a narrow amber strip pinned above everything else. On a wide screen it reads:

Top of the windowalways present
Banner Development identities only — not production authentication

On a narrow window the same strip shortens to Development identities · local only. It is announced to screen readers as a status, and it never scrolls away.

This banner is telling you three separate things.

  1. You are not authenticated. Nothing has verified who you are. There is no password, no token, and no single sign-on in this release.
  2. Your identity is a simulation. The five people you can be are fixed test personas, not accounts belonging to real colleagues.
  3. This is not a production environment. Do not put anything into RepoFluent that you would mind other people seeing.
Do not upload real proprietary source code

Because there is no authentication, anyone who can reach the address is every persona at once, including the administrator. Treat the instance as an open demonstration environment. Use the example package or content you are comfortable sharing.

Choosing your persona

In the top-right corner of the header there is a menu labelled Development persona. It has five options, and whichever one is selected is who RepoFluent thinks you are.

Header, right sidepersona selector
synced Development persona Author ▾

The synced pill beside it is decorative in this release — it always says synced and does not report anything real.

Choose To do this
Author Upload a curriculum package, watch it validate, read the receipt, and retry validation if it stalls.
Reviewer Preview a draft, acknowledge warnings, approve or reject a specific version, and compare published versions.
Administrator Publish an approved version, assign it to a learner, compare versions, and retire a version.
Auditor Read the lifecycle history and operational evidence. An auditor can change nothing at all.
Learner Open the learning assigned to you and work through courses, lessons, and source context.

How to switch

  1. Open the persona menu

    Click the menu labelled Development persona in the header, or reach it with Tab and open it with the keyboard.

  2. Pick a role

    The change takes effect immediately. There is no confirmation step, no reload, and no sign-out.

  3. Watch the page change

    The current page re-fetches its data as the new persona, so buttons appear and disappear as you switch. This is the fastest way to understand the permission model — walk one curriculum through the lifecycle, switching persona at each stage.

    What you should see Switching to Learner while looking at the Curricula page leaves only the upload panel visible. Switching to Auditor removes every action except Inspect lifecycle history.

What your persona actually controls

Your selection is remembered in your browser and attached to every request RepoFluent makes. Three consequences follow, and they explain most of the surprises people hit in their first hour.

It is per browser
The choice is stored locally in the browser you are using, under the key repofluent-development-persona. A different browser, a different profile, or a private window starts again as the default. Clearing site data resets it.
It is enforced by the server, not hidden by the page
RepoFluent does not merely hide buttons you should not press. The API refuses the action too. If you construct a request you are not entitled to make, you get a refusal rather than a result.
Everyone shares one workspace
All five personas operate inside the same single tenant. A package you upload as Author is the same package you review as Reviewer a moment later. That is what makes it possible to walk the whole lifecycle alone.

The only learner that exists

There is exactly one learner in the directory: Maya Quinn. When an administrator assigns learning, Maya is the only choice in the list. When you switch to the Learner persona, you are Maya. There is no user management, no groups, and no way to add a second learner in this release.

Preview limitation

Personas are available only when the instance is running in a development, test, or acceptance-test configuration. If an operator starts RepoFluent in any other configuration, the persona menu stops working and every protected request is refused. That is intentional: these identities must never reach a real deployment.

Where you land

Whatever persona you choose, the address opens the Curricula page — the governed content pipeline where packages are uploaded, validated, reviewed, published, and retired. If you are a learner, the page you want is Learning, one click away in the left rail.
